Research program

From genes to biomarkers to better sight

My research integrates human genetics, multi-omics, and cell biology to understand and ultimately predict the molecular events that lead to vision loss.

Our main focus

The "3N" of eye disease

Our laboratory's central theme is understanding three interconnected pathological processes, the "3N", that together drive the most common vision-threatening conditions of the retina.

Anatomy of vision

Where in the eye our research works

Scroll to follow light to the back of the eye — and see the structures where the molecular events we study take place.

The neural film

The retina

Light lands on the retina, a thin sheet of neurons lining the back of the eye. When these cells die or malfunction — neurodegeneration — vision fails. We map the genes that keep retinal neurons alive, from photoreceptors to ganglion cells.

Central, high-acuity vision

The macula

At the retina's centre, the macula gives us sharp, detailed sight. Its breakdown drives age-related macular degeneration (AMD) — a signature focus of our genetics work in Indian populations.

The cable to the brain

The optic nerve

Ganglion-cell axons bundle at the optic disc and leave the eye as the optic nerve. Their progressive loss defines glaucoma, where we study cilia-centrosomal genes and OPTN-driven cell death.

The blood supply

Retinal vasculature

A delicate vascular network feeds the retina. Leaky, abnormal vessels — neovascularization — and "metabolic memory" underlie diabetic retinopathy and ROP, where we hunt tear biomarkers and epigenetic drivers.

Core themes

Four interconnected research directions

Genetics of complex eye disease

We map the genetic architecture of age-related macular degeneration (AMD), high myopia, and glaucoma in Indian populations, identifying risk variants, candidate genes, and gene-gene interactions. Recent work implicates cilia-centrosomal genes such as CEP164 in primary congenital glaucoma and characterises OPTN mutants in retinal ganglion cell death.

GWAS · candidate genes · functional validation

Tear & vitreous biomarkers

A major focus is the discovery of non-invasive biomarkers for retinopathies, including diabetic retinopathy (DR), diabetic macular edema (DME), and retinopathy of prematurity (ROP). Using tear proteomics and lipidomics we have identified matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) as tear markers, and co-developed nanowire biosensors and chemiresistive biochips for rapid, point-of-care screening.

proteomics · lipidomics · biosensors

Neurovascular & neuro-glia biology

We investigate how neuron–glia interactions and inflammation drive neurovascular damage in the retina. This includes the role of the complement pathway in non-infectious uveitis and microglial responses to hypoxia and ischemic stress relevant to retinal degeneration.

neuro-glia · inflammation · complement

Epigenetics & metabolic memory

Diabetic vascular complications persist even after glucose control, a phenomenon called "metabolic memory." Our work links DNA methyltransferases (DNMT1) and nucleotide metabolism (IMPDH2) to sustained endothelial dysfunction in diabetic retinopathy, pointing toward new epigenetic therapeutic targets.

DNA methylation · endothelial biology
Approach

Translational, multi-omics, clinically anchored

Working within a leading eye institute gives our laboratory direct access to well-characterised patient cohorts and ocular tissues. We pair this with a broad molecular toolkit to move from observation to mechanism to clinical application.

Our methods span next-generation and deep sequencing, real-time PCR and gene-expression profiling, mass-spectrometry proteomics and lipidomics, primary human retinal cell culture, and integrated bioinformatic analysis of multi-omics data.

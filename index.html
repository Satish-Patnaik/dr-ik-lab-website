Research focus

Understanding why vision fails at the molecular level

Our lab centres on the "3N" of eye disease: neurodegeneration, neuroinflammation, and neovascularization, connecting human genetics, proteomics, and cell biology to uncover the mechanisms behind the most common blinding diseases. Explore the 3N focus →

Genetics of complex eye disease

Identifying causal genes and variants in age-related macular degeneration, high myopia, and primary congenital and open-angle glaucoma.

AMD · Myopia · Glaucoma →

Tear & vitreous biomarkers

Discovering non-invasive protein and lipid biomarkers, and biosensor platforms, for early diagnosis of diabetic retinopathy and retinopathy of prematurity.

Proteomics · Biosensors →

Neurovascular & epigenetic mechanisms

Probing neuron–glia interactions, inflammation and "metabolic memory" that drive neurovascular damage in the diabetic and degenerating retina.

About

A geneticist working at the frontier of eye disease

Dr. Inderjeet Kaur is a Scientist in Molecular Genetics at the L V Prasad Eye Institute (LVPEI), Hyderabad, based at the Kallam Anji Reddy Campus. She trained in molecular human genetics and has spent her career unravelling the molecular mechanisms of complex eye diseases.

Her research on age-related macular degeneration and myopia has been recognised as pioneering work in the field from India, supported by extramural grants from the Department of Biotechnology (DBT) and Department of Science and Technology (DST).

She is a recipient of the ARVO Young Investigator (Merit) Award, the Amjad Rahi Prize, and the B M Birla Science Medal.
